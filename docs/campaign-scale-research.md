# Campaign scope and honest scale claims

Research and inventory decision made October 3, 2026 during the authorized build. Fictitious AI remains fictional and is positioned as an AI-assisted product analytics SaaS. Mixpanel is a real competitor/reference brand; its measurements must never be presented as Fictitious AI performance.

## What the research supports

There is no established universal asset count in the sources reviewed. A launch's tier, audience, duration, channel mix, and enablement needs determine its bill of materials. The counts below are our explicit planning assumptions, not a published industry average. The user selected 104 across at least ten channels after the initial 96-item proposal; the final inventory uses eleven genuine delivery surfaces.

- [Lovable's launch tracker and tiered BOM](https://lovable.dev/blog/how-lovable-built-a-product-launch-tracker), February 25, 2026: a major launch spans landing page, email, blog, social, video, paid, sales enablement and other distribution work; smaller releases receive a much narrower scope. This supports tiering and channel breadth, not a particular count.
- [Intercom's launch retrospective](https://www.intercom.com/blog/how-to-evolve-product-launches-as-you-grow/), February 6, 2019: expanding standard checklists can create work disconnected from impact. Select activity according to the business objective and audience. We audit an existing campaign and preserve unaffected work rather than generating a new item simply to increase a count.
- [HubSpot's launch checklist](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/product-launch-checklist): marketing, product education, and sales enablement require different kinds of content. Its enumerated formats support a varied inventory, not a benchmark of 104 assets.

The scenario is one commercial change affecting an existing multiweek launch campaign. It is not a claim that every pricing update deserves 104 new promotional assets.

Each inventory row needs its own audience, purpose, distribution use and count rationale. A channel variant counts only when its editorial treatment requires a separate review. Exact copies, resizes, source files, render exports, thumbnails, captions and publisher metadata are companions of the same deliverable. A slide deck counts once, not once per slide. Archived earlier campaign versions do not count toward the active inventory.

## What we can say

After the inventory is authored and verified: "The demo campaign contains 104 distinct deliverables across eleven channels, including audience and platform variants. Metadata and exports are reviewed with their parent deliverable."

After a real run only: report registered, inspected, revised, unchanged, blocked and approved counts separately. Missing native Slides, failed renders or unresolved claims remain visible and cannot be described as finished outputs.

"Designed for campaigns with hundreds of deliverables" is an architectural intent. "Tested with hundreds" requires a measured 200+ deliverable workload with clearly identified real versus synthetic input, model, elapsed time, cost and failures. A near-100 inventory alone does not prove production scale or quality.

## Measurement

Time a stratified human sample before exposing suggested revisions: the eleven channels, including metadata and an unaffected control. Record active editing, checking and exception-review minutes separately from rendering/model wait time. Extrapolation must show sample size, channel weights, assumptions and uncertainty; it is an estimate, not a measured 104-asset baseline.

For the system, retain run timestamps, asset/field coverage, model requests and usage, unresolved findings, human review time and approved package hashes. The primary benefit to test is faster verified change completion with fewer stale or misleading claims. Visibility and revenue lift remain unmeasured.
